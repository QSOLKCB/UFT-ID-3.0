# Vopson Infodynamics Replication and Adversarial Test Matrix

## Purpose

This matrix defines a fair-test program for Melvin Vopson's published information-physics and infodynamics claims. The rule is simple:

> Reconstruct the strongest published claim exactly, reproduce it, then try to break it.

The target is the proposition, not the person.

## Claim family A: mass-energy-information equivalence

### A1. 2019 mass-equivalence principle

**Source:** *The mass-energy-information equivalence principle*, AIP Advances 9, 095206 (2019), DOI `10.1063/1.5123794`.

**Published claim to reconstruct:** a stored information bit has a finite, temperature-dependent mass, with a predicted mass difference between erased and information-filled storage states.

**Required reproduction:** derive the reported bit-mass expression from the stated Landauer and mass-energy premises with every thermodynamic step explicit.

**Adversarial tests:**

- distinguish minimum erasure work from energy stored in a logical state;
- distinguish transient dissipation from rest-mass difference;
- track temperature dependence and the choice of logical implementation;
- compare equal-energy physical encodings carrying different logical information;
- compare different encodings carrying the same logical information;
- identify whether the prediction is invariant to a reversible recoding of the bits;
- determine what experiment uniquely distinguishes an information-specific mass term from ordinary stored electromagnetic, chemical, elastic, magnetic, or thermal energy.

**Decisive result:** an experimentally measurable invariant that follows from logical information content and cannot be reduced to ordinary energy accounting.

### A2. 2022 annihilation protocol

**Source:** *Experimental protocol for testing the mass-energy-information equivalence principle*, AIP Advances 12, 035311 (2022), DOI `10.1063/5.0087175`.

**Published claim to reconstruct:** electron-positron annihilation should include additional low-energy photons associated with erasure of particle information content.

**Adversarial tests:**

- reproduce the predicted spectral wavelength and energy budget;
- establish conservation accounting without double-counting particle rest energy;
- model Standard Model backgrounds and detector sensitivity;
- specify whether the proposed signal depends on environmental temperature and, if so, why the intrinsic annihilation outcome should do so;
- define a preregistered null result and exclusion region.

## Claim family B: genomic information entropy

### B1. 2021 GENIES method

**Source:** Vopson & Robson, *A new method to study genome mutations using the information entropy*, Physica A 584, 126383 (2021), DOI `10.1016/j.physa.2021.126383`.

**Published method:** fixed nucleotide windows and m-block Shannon information-entropy spectra are used to compare sequences and detect mutations.

**Required reproduction:** reimplement the algorithm from the paper, validate against the reported SARS-CoV-2 sequences, and confirm mutation detection.

**Adversarial tests:**

- sweep block size and window length;
- test boundary alignment and overlapping versus non-overlapping windows;
- compare with simple Hamming/edit-distance baselines;
- use shuffled and composition-matched null genomes;
- determine whether the entropy spectrum adds predictive information beyond local nucleotide frequencies;
- run held-out viral families and nonviral genomes.

### B2. 2022 proposed mutation law

**Source:** *A possible information entropic law of genetic mutations*, Applied Sciences 12, 6912 (2022), DOI `10.3390/app12146912`.

**Published claim to reconstruct:** genome mutation dynamics exhibit a tendency to reduce overall information entropy.

**Adversarial tests:**

- separate mutation generation from natural selection and sampling of surviving lineages;
- use phylogenetically appropriate null models;
- compare synonymous, nonsynonymous, neutral, deleterious, and beneficial mutation classes where data permit;
- test ascertainment bias from reference-sequence choice;
- preregister entropy measure and windowing before lineage analysis;
- evaluate whether sequence-length, GC-content, codon-bias, or population bottlenecks explain the trend;
- test prospective rather than retrospective prediction.

**Decisive result:** a robust entropy tendency that predicts mutation outcomes out of sample after standard evolutionary mechanisms and sequence-composition effects are controlled.

## Claim family C: Second Law of Infodynamics

### C1. 2022 Second Law of Information Dynamics

**Source:** Vopson & Lepadatu, AIP Advances 12, 075310 (2022), DOI `10.1063/5.0100358`.

**Published claim to reconstruct:** for the information systems examined under a Shannon-information framework, information entropy remains constant or decreases with time, proposed as a second law of information dynamics.

**Core audit:** reconstruct exactly what counts as a state, event alphabet, probability distribution, physical boundary, and time-evolution rule in each example.

**Adversarial tests:**

- reversible permutations;
- deliberately mixing Markov processes;
- deterministic structure-generation processes;
- open systems with information inflow;
- lossless recodings;
- changes of alphabet and partition;
- coarse-graining sweeps;
- entropy of state versus entropy rate;
- Shannon entropy versus relative entropy and mutual information;
- finite-size and estimator bias.

**Primary UFT-ID question:** is non-increase a universal law, a restricted theorem under contraction/coarse-graining assumptions, or a property of the chosen examples and encodings?

## Claim family D: 2023 cross-domain extension

**Source:** *The second law of infodynamics and its implications for the simulated universe hypothesis*, AIP Advances 13, 105308 (2023), DOI `10.1063/5.0173278`.

The paper extends the proposed law across digital information, genetics, atomic physics, mathematical symmetry, and cosmology and then argues that the collection of results supports a simulated-universe interpretation.

### D1. Digital information

Reproduce compression examples and distinguish:

```text
description-length minimization
```

from

```text
physical-state entropy reduction.
```

Test whether a different lossless code reverses or removes the apparent trend while preserving the same logical content.

### D2. Atomic physics and Hund-rule examples

Reconstruct the microstate counting and compare with standard quantum-statistical and energetic explanations. Determine whether the infodynamic quantity predicts an independently measurable result not already fixed by the underlying physics.

### D3. Mathematical symmetry

Distinguish a theorem about a selected multiplicity descriptor from a dynamical law of nature. A static combinatorial extremum is not automatically temporal dynamics.

### D4. Cosmology

State exactly what ensemble, coarse-graining, horizon, and entropy are used. Compare with standard thermodynamic and gravitational entropy accounting.

### D5. Simulation-hypothesis inference

Treat this as a separate inference step. Even if every entropy example reproduces, test whether

```text
entropy optimization -> computational universe
```

is unique, necessary, or merely one interpretation among conventional physical explanations.

## Claim family E: gravity as information compression

### E1. 2025 gravity derivation

**Source:** *Is gravity evidence of a computational universe?*, AIP Advances 15, 045035 (2025), DOI `10.1063/5.0264945`.

**Published claim to reconstruct:** Newtonian gravitational attraction is derived as an entropic information force associated with reducing information entropy/data compression.

**Line-by-line audit:**

- dimensions of every intermediate quantity;
- dependence on particle number, separation, volume, surface area, and discretization;
- any assumption that already contains inverse-square geometry;
- choice of spatial cells or information-bearing degrees of freedom;
- dependence on coordinate system and encoding;
- use of mass-energy-information equivalence as an independent premise;
- whether the derivation is circular or genuinely predictive.

**Comparative tests:**

- Newtonian two-body systems;
- extended mass distributions;
- shell theorem cases;
- weak-field limits with non-spherical geometry;
- equivalence-principle requirements;
- comparison with Verlinde-style entropic gravity;
- identify predictions that differ from Newtonian gravity or GR.

**Decisive result:** a novel quantitative prediction or correction term that is not algebraically inserted through the counting assumptions and can be tested independently.

## Claim family F: symmetry

### F1. 2026 Euclidean polygons

**Source:** *The Role of Information Entropy in Symmetry of Euclidean Polygons*, Entropy 28, 564 (2026), DOI `10.3390/e28050564`.

**Published result to reconstruct:** within the paper's fixed-size polygon descriptor, maximum symmetry corresponds to a Shannon-entropy minimum.

**Adversarial tests:**

- derive the result directly from multiplicity constraints;
- compare alternative symmetry descriptors;
- check label/permutation invariance;
- test whether entropy minimum follows tautologically from the chosen category construction;
- separate static extremum from temporal evolution;
- construct asymmetric polygons with identical chosen entropy where possible;
- test whether the information measure has discriminative power beyond standard group/symmetry measures.

## Claim family G: language diversity

### G1. 2026 language-diversity decline

**Source:** Kadin & Vopson, *Second law of info-dynamics and the language diversity decline*, Journal of Quantitative Linguistics (early online, 18 July 2026).

**Published program to reconstruct:** language population dynamics are modeled and a decline in total information entropy is interpreted as consistent with the second law of infodynamics.

**Adversarial tests:**

- reproduce the dynamical model and dataset;
- compare Shannon entropy against standard diversity indices;
- determine whether entropy decline is a mathematical consequence of category extinction or dominance in the underlying demographic model;
- test historical intervals with increasing language diversity or fragmentation where usable data exist;
- perform sensitivity analysis to classification of languages versus dialects;
- compare population-weighted and unweighted formulations;
- determine whether infodynamics adds predictive content beyond the population dynamics already assumed.

## Cross-cutting tests

Every claim family should run the same audit where mathematically relevant:

```text
1. Exact source reproduction
2. Unit/dimension audit
3. Boundary and ensemble audit
4. Representation/alphabet/partition sweep
5. Null model
6. Alternative established explanation
7. Held-out or prospective test
8. Uncertainty and multiple-comparison control
9. Explicit counterexample search
10. Unique-prediction test
```

## What would count as a strong UFT-ID result?

The goal is not to make every Vopson result false. A stronger outcome is a classification theorem or empirical map showing precisely which claims survive.

Possible outcomes include:

- **valid restricted theorem**;
- **encoding-dependent effect**;
- **coarse-graining effect**;
- **standard data-processing result**;
- **empirical regularity with limited scope**;
- **failed replication**;
- **counterexample to universality**;
- **interpretation not uniquely supported**;
- **novel surviving prediction**.

If Vopson's strongest claim survives the battery, UFT-ID must say so.
